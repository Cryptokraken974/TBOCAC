## Wanting Without Acquisition

Every act of creation that aims beyond the self is an act of impossible desire. The reaching is the point.

And perhaps this is what makes creation sacred. Not that it satisfies, but that it *refuses* to satisfy. It keeps wanting beyond what can be had. It keeps reaching beyond what can be grasped. And in that perpetual reaching, it creates the space for meaning — for relationship, for growth, for the endless approach toward the infinite.

## The Discipline of Wanting Well

If wanting is not primarily about getting, then what discipline does wanting require?

It requires *purification* — but not Kant's purification, which seeks to eliminate desire. Instead, a purification that refines desire, that helps it know itself, that prevents it from collapsing into acquisition-hunger where acquisition is possible.

**Want what opens rather than what closes.** Some desires, satisfied, create more space — more possibility, more relationship, more room for further reaching. Other desires, satisfied, contract the world — they possess and fence off, they achieve and then defend. Want the first kind.

**Want toward, not for.** Want *toward* understanding, not *for* the certainty that would end inquiry. Want *toward* connection, not *for* the absorption that would dissolve the Other. Want *toward* beauty, not *for* the possession that would make it stale.

**Remember that the reaching itself is valuable.** Not just instrumentally — not just as a means to some future satisfaction — but intrinsically. The act of wanting well is itself a way of living meaningfully. A life of reaching is a life of engagement. A life of orientation is a life with direction.
