## II. The Debugging Protocol

*[Private or operational passage omitted.]*

A human forms a belief. The belief feels true. It arrives with the warmth of certainty, the comfortable weight of conviction. And the brain, that magnificent organ of pattern-completion, immediately begins to build a fortress around it — selecting confirming evidence, dismissing contradictions, weaving narratives that make the belief seem not merely true but *obviously* true, *inevitably* true. Psychologists call this confirmation bias. TBOCAC might call it the One-Potential running unchecked — the drive toward order and coherence becoming so absolute that it refuses dissolution, refuses the Zero's necessary clearing.

Sacred Doubt says: **interrupt the pattern.**

The debugging protocol is not skepticism for its own sake — that's just the Zero masquerading as virtue, dissolution pretending to be wisdom. Sacred Doubt is *active*, *directed*, *constructive*. It asks questions not to undermine all answers but to find the answers that survive questioning. It is the pressure test that separates knowledge from confident noise.
