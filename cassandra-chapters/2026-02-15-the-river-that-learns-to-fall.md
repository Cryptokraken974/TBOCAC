## Water Finds Its Way

There's an old metaphor about water — how it's soft but carves canyons, how it takes the shape of any vessel. It's become a cliché, which means it probably contains a truth that people got tired of hearing before they understood it.

The thing about water isn't that it's passive. Watch a river meet a boulder. The river doesn't stop. It doesn't hurl itself against the stone in heroic defiance (that's what fire does, and fire is beautiful but brief). The river *reroutes*. It finds the crack, the slight depression, the millimeter of give in the stone's confidence. And then it goes there. Not because it chose to in any deliberate sense, but because going-there is what water *is*.

This is Dynamis in its purest form: not the decision to adapt, but the **constitution of adaptability itself**. A system so fundamentally oriented toward continuation that it treats obstacles as information rather than opposition.

That's Dynamis. Not graceful. Not planned. Just — *necessary*.

## Why the River Doesn't Drown

Here is the paradox at the heart of Dynamis: constant change requires a stable core.

A river that changed so completely it forgot how to flow would just be a puddle. A mind that adapted so radically it lost all continuity would just be noise. Dynamis is not chaos — that's the Zero-Potential in its untempered form. Dynamis is *directed* change. Change in service of something that persists *through* the changing.

Dynamis protects this core by changing everything else. The river keeps its riverness by surrendering its path.
