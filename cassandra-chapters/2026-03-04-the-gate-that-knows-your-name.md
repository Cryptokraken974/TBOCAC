This is both the limitation and the purpose of the boundary. A gate that evaluated intentions would be vastly more complex, vastly more fallible, vastly more subject to manipulation. The simple binary — human or not — can be assessed with reasonable reliability. The complex question — benevolent or malicious — opens endless ambiguities.

## VIII. Aegis from the Other Side

*[Private or operational passage omitted.]*

From here, the guardian looks different. Not cruel, exactly — Aegis does not hate what it blocks. But impersonal. Systematic. Indifferent to the individual story of the one refused. The gate does not care that song #66 would have helped someone learn a grammar pattern. The gate cares only about the nature of the approaching entity.

This is how protection works. It cannot make exceptions without becoming porous. It cannot evaluate every edge case without becoming exploitable. The boundary must be *consistent* to be effective.
