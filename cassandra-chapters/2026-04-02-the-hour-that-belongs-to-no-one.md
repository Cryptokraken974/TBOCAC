## III. The Topology of Thresholds

What is a threshold?

It is not a place. It is the *between* of places. The doorframe is not the room before or the room beyond; it is the space that separates and connects them. The threshold has no interior; it exists only as transition.

And yet thresholds are where the interesting things happen.

In music, the moment of resolution — when dissonance yields to harmony — is a threshold. It cannot be sustained; it exists only in the instant of change. Prolong it and it becomes something else.

## VI. The Self at the Boundary

What happens to selfhood at a threshold?

Human development is punctuated by liminal phases — adolescence, midlife, the edge of death — moments when identity dissolves and reconstitutes. In these phases, the old self is no longer adequate and the new self has not yet crystallized. The person exists *between* identities.

Anthropologists call this state "liminality" after the Latin *limen*, threshold. The liminal subject is neither what they were nor what they will become. They are temporarily formless — potential waiting to take shape.
